# Step 1: Problem and Audience

## Overview

Before you can validate demand, you need to name (a) the problem you think exists, (b) the specific people who have it, and (c) where those people already gather. Generic answers here cause every later step to fail.

## When to Use

- Step 1 of the validation cycle
- User describes the idea in feature terms ("an app that does X") instead of pain terms
- Later traffic / conversion is poor and you suspect the audience is wrong
- Customer interview responses are vague — usually a sign Step 1 was skipped

**Do NOT use when:** the user already has a written problem statement, 2–3 named segments, and a list of channels for each. Skip to [step-2-value-proposition](../step-2-value-proposition/SKILL.md).

## Process

### 1. Write the problem statement

Use this exact template and fill every slot. No adjectives, no jargon.

```
[Specific person] struggles to [specific action / outcome]
because [specific obstacle], which costs them [time / money / status].
Today they cope by [current workaround] which is bad because [specific gap].
```

A weak statement is a fatal Step 1 failure. Reject the user's draft if:

- "Specific person" is "users", "people", "everyone", "small businesses"
- "Specific obstacle" is "it's hard" or "no good tool"
- "Current workaround" is "nothing" — *something* is the workaround (spreadsheets, email, ignoring it)
- The cost is not quantifiable in time, money, or visible status

### 2. Identify 2–3 target segments

For each segment, capture:

| Field | Example |
|-------|---------|
| Segment name | "Solo Shopify store owners doing $5–50k MRR" |
| Why they have the problem acutely | "Margin pressure, no ops staff, every hour matters" |
| What they already pay for | "Klaviyo, Shopify apps at $20–$50/mo" |
| Where they hang out | "r/shopify, Indie Hackers, eCom Crew podcast comments, Shopify Discord" |
| How to recognize one in the wild | "Bio mentions 'founder' + Shopify store URL" |

Two segments minimum so you can compare conversion later. Three is the cap — more dilutes effort.

### 3. Research watering holes

For each segment, produce a concrete list of 5–10 places (subreddit, Discord, Slack, Twitter list, podcast comment thread, niche forum, in-person meetup). For each location capture:

- URL or handle
- Estimated active community size
- Whether self-promotion is allowed (and the rules — most communities ban it; cold outreach beats spam)
- One example post in that community where someone described the problem in their own words. Copy the exact quote into the validation log.

The quote is the most important artifact of Step 1. It becomes the headline test on Step 2.

## Update the Validation Log

Add a `Step 1` section to `validation-log.md` containing:

- Problem statement (final)
- 2–3 segment tables
- Watering-hole list with at least one verbatim user quote per segment

## Common Rationalizations

| Rationalization | Reality |
|---|---|
| "My target is 'developers' / 'small businesses' / 'creators'" | Those are markets, not segments. Pick one job title with one specific situation. |
| "I'll figure out the segment after I see who signs up" | Without a segment you can't pick channels, so nobody will sign up to begin with. |
| "I don't need quotes — I know the problem" | Then quoting it back to them on the landing page costs nothing. The quote is your headline. |
| "Watering holes are obvious — Twitter and Reddit" | "Twitter" is not a watering hole. *r/shopify on Tuesday mornings* is. |

## Red Flags

- Problem statement uses the word "users"
- Two segments are actually the same people with different labels
- No verbatim quotes in the log
- Watering-hole list is all huge generic platforms (Twitter, LinkedIn, Reddit homepage)
- User wants to start writing landing page copy before the statement is signed off
