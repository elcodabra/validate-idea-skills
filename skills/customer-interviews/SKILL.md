# Customer Interviews

## Overview

A short discovery interview that proves whether a problem is real by asking about past behavior, not future intent. Used as a sub-skill from Steps 1, 5, and 6 of the 7-step validation. Heavily inspired by *The Mom Test* (Rob Fitzpatrick).

## When to Use

- Step 1 — to validate the problem statement with people in the segment
- Step 5 — every reply to outreach that says "interesting" → invite to a 15-min call
- Step 6 — every decline of the pre-sell — ask why, in person
- Anytime someone wants to "do user research" but their script is full of "would you" questions

## Process

### 1. Three rules

1. **Talk about their life, not your idea.** Don't pitch. The moment you describe the product, the data is contaminated.
2. **Ask about specifics from the past, not generics about the future.** "When did you last...?" beats "Would you...?"
3. **Talk less, listen more.** If you're talking >30% of the call, you're pitching.

### 2. The script (15 min)

Adapt verbatim:

```
0:00 Hi, thanks for the time. I'm researching how [segment]
     handle [problem area] — nothing to sell. Mind if I ask
     a few questions about how you do it today?

2:00 When was the last time you ran into [problem]?
     Walk me through what happened.

5:00 What did you try? What tools / workarounds did you reach for?
     What did and didn't work?

8:00 How often does this come up? How big a deal is it relative
     to other stuff on your plate?

10:00 If you had a magic wand, what would the ideal fix look like?
      (Listen — don't propose your product.)

12:00 Who else have you seen struggle with this? Could you intro me?

14:00 Anything I should have asked but didn't?
```

Only after the call, with their permission, share the landing page link.

### 3. Bad questions to never ask

| Don't ask | Why | Ask instead |
|-----------|-----|-------------|
| "Would you use this?" | People say yes to be nice | "When did you last need this? What did you do?" |
| "Do you think this is a good idea?" | Opinion, not behavior | "How are you solving this today?" |
| "Would you pay for it?" | Free is easy to promise | "What do you pay for in this space today?" |
| "How much would you pay?" | They're guessing | "What does your current workaround cost in time or money?" |

### 4. Score the conversation

After each call, log:

- Did they describe a **specific recent instance** of the problem? (Y/N — N = problem may not be real)
- Did they **already pay for** something adjacent? (Y/N — Y = budget exists)
- Did they offer an **intro** to others with the same problem? (Y/N — Y = strong signal)
- Did they ask **when they could use the product**, unprompted? (Y/N — Y = strongest signal)

3–4 yeses across ≥5 interviews = strong qualitative GO contribution.

### 5. Quotes go in the validation log

Capture **verbatim** quotes, not summaries. Verbatim user language goes into headlines, ads, and outreach. Paraphrases lose the signal.

## Common Rationalizations

| Rationalization | Reality |
|---|---|
| "I'll just send a survey, it's faster" | Surveys produce leading-question vanity data. 5 calls > 500 survey rows. |
| "I'll show them my landing page on the call to get reaction" | Then you're testing your pitch, not learning their problem. Save the page for after the call. |
| "I'll skip interviews because I am the customer" | You are a sample of one with strong confirmation bias. Talk to others. |
| "30 min is more thorough than 15" | 15 forces focus and respects calendars. People say yes to 15. |

## Red Flags

- More than 30% of speaking time is yours
- Any sentence starting with "Wouldn't it be cool if..."
- No verbatim quotes in the log
- Skipping the "intro to others" ask (this compounds the channel later)
- Interviewing only friends and family

## Verification

- [ ] ≥5 interviews completed with people in the Step 1 segment (not friends, not investors)
- [ ] Each scored on the 4 questions in section 4 (Score the conversation); ≥3 yeses across ≥5 interviews fed into the Step 7 verdict
- [ ] Verbatim quotes captured in the validation log
- [ ] At least one intro asked for per call
